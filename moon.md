# the moon and our tides

The ocean is always being gently pulled. The moon's gravity grabs the water
and heaps it up into a bulge on the side of the earth facing the moon. On the
opposite side of the earth, the water gets a little left behind as the earth
is tugged away from it, so a second bulge forms there too.

The earth spins all the way around once a day, and our beach rides through
both bulges. Riding through a bulge is high tide. Riding through the low
water between them is low tide. Two bulges means most days get two highs and
two lows.

## why some days have bigger tides

The sun pulls on the ocean too, about half as hard as the moon. When the
moon and sun line up (full moon or new moon), they pull together and the
tides get extra big: higher highs and lower lows. When the moon is half lit,
they pull in different directions and sort of cancel, so the tides get lazy.

That is what the moon faces on the stay page tell you. Round bright moon:
big dramatic tides. Half moon: smaller, gentler tides.

## why our two low tides are so different

Here is the sneaky part. The moon does not sit over the equator. Every month
it wanders north of it, then south of it, like someone slowly walking up and
down a hill. That walk is why our moon pictures ride above and below the
dotted line.

When the moon is far north, its water bulge is aimed north of the equator
too. Our beach spins through the fat middle of one bulge and only clips the
skinny edge of the other one. So one low tide drops way down, and the other
low barely drops at all. When the moon walks back near the line, our two
lows become almost twins again.

## our strait's secret

Most beaches barely notice any of this, because their twice-a-day tide is
huge and drowns it out. Our water is special. The twice-a-day tide sneaks
into Northumberland Strait from both ends at once, and the two waves arrive
out of step -- like two people pushing a swing at opposite moments, they
mostly cancel each other out.

With the twice-a-day tide hushed, the once-a-day pull gets to shine. That is
why the difference between our two lows is so easy to see here, and why our
beach is one of the best places anywhere to watch the moon's walk written
in the sand.

## what to watch this week

On the stay page, watch the moons climb higher above the dotted line each
day. The higher they climb, the further apart the two lows drift. Only the
deep lows (the small numbers on the curve) uncover the really big sand
flats -- those are the afternoons to bring the camera.
